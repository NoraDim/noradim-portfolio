The gap

Most people don't lack information. They lack a corrected starting point.

Ask most beginners what Web3 is and you'll get a confident, half-right answer: "it's crypto," "you need to code," "it's a scam." That's not a knowledge gap, it's a misconception sitting where knowledge should be. Teaching fundamentals on top of that without addressing it first just reinforces the wrong model with new vocabulary.

This program was built to fix the starting point before building on it: correct the misconception, then lay the fundamentals, then prove it with practice.

How I think about explaining hard ideas

Context before jargon

Complex ideas don't stick when they're just defined, they stick when they're placed somewhere. Rather than introducing Web3 as a standalone buzzword, I show it as the next stage in a progression people already understand, the web evolving from static, to interactive, to what's emerging now. The label comes after the context, not before it.

The web's evolution used to frame Web3 as a continuation, not a mystery.
In plain terms

A few of the concepts this program builds from

Not the full curriculum, just a sense of how the ideas get explained inside it.

Foundation

Blockchain

A record-keeping system with no single owner. Instead of one company holding the master copy, thousands of independent computers each hold a matching one, and they all have to agree before anything gets added.

Foundation

Decentralization

Moving control away from one central authority and spreading it across a wider network. It's the design choice that makes blockchain possible, not a feature bolted on afterward.

Application

Smart contracts

An agreement that carries out itself. When the conditions written into it are met, it executes automatically, no one has to chase a signature or trust the other side to follow through.

Application

DAOs

Organizations where the rules of decision-making are built into the system itself, rather than sitting with a board or a boss. Members participate in governance directly, and the rules are visible to everyone, not just management.
