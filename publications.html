Towards a Mobile Drugs Authentication System for Nigerian Users

A research paper proposing a smartphone based redesign of Nigeria's drug verification system, written during my BSc at the University of Benin, with the fake drug crisis in Nigeria as its starting point rather than the technology itself.

Field
Mobile healthcare systems
Institution
University of Benin, Dept. of Computer Science
My role
Co-author
Year
2015
The problem

Fake drugs were a matter of life and death, and the existing check was clumsy

Counterfeit medication has been a serious and ongoing crisis in Nigeria, contributing to thousands of deaths each year. By the time this paper was written, NAFDAC already ran a Mobile Authentication Service that let buyers verify a drug over SMS or USSD. It worked, and it reached people regardless of phone type or data access, but it was basic and unfriendly to use, a holdover from an earlier era of much lower internet penetration.

The paper's premise: the underlying need, fast and trustworthy drug verification, hadn't changed, but the tool for meeting it had fallen behind what users had come to expect from a modern app.

What we proposed

Drug Checker: the same verification, rebuilt as a native mobile experience

We designed a hybrid mobile application, Drug Checker, that replaced the SMS exchange with a proper interface: a home screen, input fields, and a result screen, all built to return an answer in 30 seconds or less. Instead of texting a code and waiting for a reply, a user types the drug name, a scratched PIN, and the NAFDAC number directly into the app.

Data layer

Centralized database

A MySQL database managed through a content management system, with drug records exposed in JSON so the mobile app could query it directly.

Interface

Guided verification flow

A single home screen collects the drug's details, checks them against valid records, and returns either full drug information or a clear invalid result.

Response path

Reporting built in

When a drug doesn't match a valid record, the user is prompted on the spot to report it and where it was purchased, so a failed check becomes an action, not a dead end.

Platform

Cross platform by design

Built with hybrid tooling (Java, GWT, Apache Cordova) so the same codebase could ship to Android first, then Windows Phone and iOS.

How verification works

From launch to result, in ten steps

1

Open the Drug Checker app.

2

Enter the drug name, the scratched PIN, and the NAFDAC number.

3

Submit for verification.

4

The app checks whether both the PIN and the NAFDAC number match a valid record.

5

If they match, full drug details are displayed.

6

If they don't, the user is told the drug does not exist and is prompted to report it.

7

The user chooses whether to file a report.

8

If yes, they enter details of the drug and where it was bought.

9

The report is sent to a dedicated address used to track counterfeit activity.

10

Exit, or return home to verify another drug.

Where it sat

Part of a wider pattern, not a first attempt

The paper situates Drug Checker alongside earlier anti-counterfeit efforts: mPedigree in Ghana, a comparable SMS based scheme in India, and NAFDAC's own Sproxil powered service in Nigeria from 2010. Rather than proposing something unprecedented, the contribution was narrower and more practical: take a verification model that already worked and rebuild its interface for a smartphone-literate generation, without giving up the accessibility that made the SMS/USSD original useful in the first place.
